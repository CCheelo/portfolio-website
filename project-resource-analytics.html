Resource Analytics Web App

2026 · Built for AB Bank Zambia's Admin team Python · SQLite · openpyxl Built · not yet in use

An analytics app that imports fuel-card, vehicle-tracking and print data, measures fuel and paper use against branch business growth, checks every fuel purchase against official ERB prices, and produces a monthly Excel/PDF pack.

Interactive Demo Summary Overview

Summary Overview

The real app and its data belong to AB Bank Zambia. The demo is my own recreation on a fictional “Demo Bank” with invented figures — nothing from the bank is published.

The question

Is business growth explaining our fuel and paper use? If a bank opens more accounts and registers more loans, it should print more and drive more. The app answers whether the resources are rising faster or slower than the business — branch by branch and vehicle by vehicle.

How the numbers work

  • Growth compares the average of the first three months with the last three, so one odd month doesn't swing the verdict. Missing months are missing, never zero.
  • Paper is judged against all business events (accounts opened + loans registered); fuel against loans, because it mostly takes officers to loan clients.
  • Km per litre is card litres ÷ tracker kilometres, counted only in months where a vehicle has both.
  • Why spend changed — each month's change in fuel spend is split into a volume effect (buying more or less fuel) and a price effect (the regulator moving the pump price).
  • Year-end forecast — recent litres × the latest announced price.

Fuel checks

Card fills are compared with what the vehicle tracker saw and with the official price: a vehicle that didn't move on the day it was filled, a fill in the middle of a tracked trip, several fills in one day, a fill bigger than the tank, a month far below the vehicle's usual km/L, and card plates with no tracker (with a suggestion when a plate is one character off). A flag is a question to ask, not proof of misuse.

Design and privacy

  • Costs nothing to run: Python's standard library, SQLite and openpyxl — no external services and no CDN.
  • Imports recognise each export by its headers, preview before saving, and handle overlapping re-imports and reversal rows safely.
  • Driver and attendant names are never imported, and card numbers are reduced to their last four digits.
  • A sidebar analytics layout with a clickable KPI strip, tabbed breakdowns, light and dark themes, and a colour-blind-safe chart palette kept separate from the brand colour.

Status

Built and tested, not yet in use. The demo above runs the same analytics rules on invented data: try the period filter, click the KPIs to drive the chart, and move the sliders in the savings calculator.
