NorthWind A/B Testing Lab

2026 · FSI Outsourcing training Excel · Hypothesis testing Complete

Did a personalised email campaign really raise order values, or was it chance? A two-sample t-test on 120 orders for the fictional NorthWind Outfitters — with the business reading as well as the statistics.

Summary Overview

Summary Overview

333.36average order — standard email (n = 60)
+11.8%lift, one-tail p = 0.035

The test

H0: personalised emails do not raise average order value. Because the two groups had different variances, I used Welch's t-test (two samples, unequal variances): t = 1.83 on 114 degrees of freedom, against a one-tail critical value of 1.66. The one-tail p-value is 0.035, so at α = 0.05 we reject H0 — the lift is unlikely to be random.

Average order value by region

The lift holds in the North, East and West, but the South went the other way (345.67 personalised vs 359.74 standard). With only a handful of orders per region, that is a reason to look closer before rolling out everywhere, not a reason to ignore the overall result.

Statistical vs practical significance

The two-tail p-value is 0.07, so the conclusion depends on having decided in advance that we only care whether personalisation helps. The practical case is the 11.8% lift in order value — large enough to matter if personalisation is cheap to run.
