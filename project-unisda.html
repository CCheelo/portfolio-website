UNISDA Strategic Planning & Reporting System

2026 · Built for University SDA Church Django · PostgreSQL · Tailwind Rolling out · users in training

A planning and reporting platform for one of Zambia's largest Adventist congregations (2,000+ members), where all 36 departments plan activities against strategic KPIs, report with quarterly scoring, and take their budgets and payment requests through approval — replacing paper forms and a stack of Excel workbooks.

The problem

The church ran its strategic plan on Excel: each department filled in a scorecard template, and the planning committee consolidated them every quarter by pasting between workbooks. Money moved on a paper Payment Request form with four signatures. Nobody could easily see who had reported, what had slipped, or how much of a budget was really left.

What it does

  • The scorecard — theme → objective → KPI → main activity → sub-activity, with budgeted beside spent, exported to Excel and PDF in the church's own template layout.
  • Quarterly reporting — each report records achievement, actual spend and three narratives (achievements, challenges, recommendations). Status is derived from achievement rather than chosen, so the two can never drift apart.
  • Money — the annual budget round, payment requests with line items and the four-stage signing chain, a monthly committee sitting that approves, reduces, varies or declines each line, trust funds, disbursements and receipts.
  • Oversight — a one-page board view, the quarterly consolidation that used to be pasted together by hand, delivery against planned timing, submission tracking, and a drafted committee commentary.
  • Workbook upload — departments that still prefer Excel upload the template; every row is previewed before anything changes, and a load can be taken back out.
  • Access — what a person sees is the union of the offices they hold and the departments they serve, both recorded per year, so a request endorsed in 2026 still names the elder who endorsed it after the rotation.

Decisions it is built on

  • Nothing is enforced that the committee should decide. A request over budget or outside its planned quarter is flagged with the figures beside it — and submitted anyway.
  • Receipts are chased, never blocking. As the church put it: “This is still church after all, we can't be business strict.”
  • The church year turns over in November, not January, so for two months a department runs two years at once — the old team reporting while the new team plans.

Tech

  • Django 5.1 with a composed permission model (predicates, not role names), PostgreSQL in production and SQLite for development.
  • A Tailwind-built design system in the church's colours, phone-first for the three screens departments use, checked at 375px.
  • Google sign-in, object storage for quotations and receipts, Excel/PDF exports, and a fast parallel test suite covering access control on every route.

Status

Deployed and rolling out: department users are in training after three rounds of user testing. The interactive demo above recreates the main screens on invented data — switch between a department leader, SPEMC and Treasury at the top right to see how access changes what each person can do.
